The menu, by the fire.

We don't sort dinner into starters and mains. We sort it by what the fire does: char, roast, smoke, and what's left in the coals. Find your heat, then settle in.

Char

Straight over the hottest part of the fire. This is the loud end of the menu: dark edges, a little crackle, a lot of heat.

Char is flavor, not a mistake. The outside goes bitter-sweet and smoky while the inside gets a chance to stay tender.

Roast

Slower, with the heat all around. The fire doesn't take requests, so roasting is where we let it work at its own pace.

It's the warm, comforting side of the menu, and it suits the nights when Portland turns cool and the candles come out.

Smoke

Wood smoke does the seasoning that salt can't. It starts quiet and ends up being what you remember.

It's the same fire that cooks your dinner, so the flavor comes from the wood. Smoke is rich, so we like something bright beside it.

From the coals

What's left when the flames settle. Glowing, gentle heat for the finishing touches at the end of the night.

Coals hold a soft, even heat long after the flames die down, which makes them the right place for slow finishing.

Season

This page is about how we cook. The dishes themselves change with the season, so what's on the fire depends on the day you come.
